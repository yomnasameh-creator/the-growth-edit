Business Basics

The simple foundations that make marketing work: customers, offers, pricing and planning.

Why the basics come first

Marketing cannot fix a business that is unclear about who it serves or what it sells. Before spending time on channels and campaigns, it helps to answer a few plain questions. Who is my customer? What problem do I solve for them? What do I offer, and what does it cost?

This topic covers those foundations for small business owners and anyone starting out. Getting them right makes every later decision easier, from the words on your website to the platforms you choose.

When you are ready to turn these answers into action, read How to Create a Marketing Plan for a Small Business. You can also explore branding and the main digital marketing channels.

  • Know your customer

    Understand who they are, what they need and where they look for help.

  • Be clear on your offer

    Say what you provide and why it matters in a sentence or two.

  • Keep track of the numbers

    Know what comes in, what goes out and what each sale really costs you.

The essentials

Foundations every small business needs

  • Customer understanding

    Talk to real customers when you can. Their own words are the best source of marketing language.

  • A clear offer

    A product or service, its benefits and who it is for, described without jargon.

  • Pricing

    Prices should cover your costs, reflect the value you provide and make sense to your customers.

  • Simple record keeping

    Track income and expenses from the start so you can make decisions with facts, not guesses.

  • Planning and goals

    Set a small number of goals you can measure and review them regularly.

  • Customer care

    Helpful, honest service encourages repeat business and word of mouth.

A note on advice: this content is general education. For decisions about taxes, legal requirements or finances, speak with a qualified professional in your area.
